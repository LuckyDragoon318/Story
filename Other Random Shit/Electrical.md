I do have some notes about it though. (this might be disorganized but I'll try to keep it in order that you would go through the website)
- A battery spilling is not a common occurrence, you should know about it, but it probably isn't going to happen
- Don't touch the positive and negatives wires or terminals together, its bad for you, and the electronics
- When you're doing anything involving metal bits, covers electronics with a rag or something
- Attaching wiring to the robot frame is great for wire management, which mill make your life way easier if something breaks, make sure that the copper in the wire is never exposed to the frame though, this is dangerous, and will cause you to fail inspection at comps
- Voltage - almost everything on your robot is 12 volts, if it isn't, it'll probably be 5 volts, and need a voltage regulator module, these are pretty simple to use, connect it to the Power Distribution Board, and then connect the labeled output to your device
- Amps - most of your motors will use a 40 amp breaker, other electronics will probably use a 5 or 10 amp breaker. If you aren't sure, try looking through the pit inspection checklist, or the game manual
- I have only used Ohms when there is a CAN issue, and it is basically just "is there a signal or not?"
- Pulling wires taught is generally not good, leave some extra
- Power cables should be red for positive and black for negative, this is the most common standard, and if it is inconsistent thats bad
- CAN wires, you should keep them twisted together when you can, because there is some electrical interference, and this helps the interference be equal between the wires - I don't know when you would need to terminate a CAN bus, since it should go to every device, and end up with every wires at one device
- PWM - the team I'm on barely uses this, I think in 2026 we only used it for LED's
- I2C - I have no idea what this is ¯\_(ツ)_/¯ 
- AWG Wires are goofy, smaller number is a bigger wire
